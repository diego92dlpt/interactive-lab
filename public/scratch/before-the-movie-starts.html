Before the movie starts

Five names,
five different jobs

A field guide to the company logos that flash by after the lights go down and before the movie actually begins.

You've counted them too: sometimes it's four or five separate company names before a single frame of story shows up. It looks repetitive, but it usually isn't — each one is a different job on the same film. Here's what each role actually means, roughly in the order they tend to appear, counted down like the reel they used to splice in front of the print.

The lineup

5

Role · Distributor / Studio

Gets it into theaters

This company didn't necessarily make the film. It releases it — pays for the trailers and billboards, books the theaters, and picks the opening weekend. It's usually the first and biggest logo you see, sometimes with no text at all.

"UNIVERSAL PICTURES" / just the logo animation, no words

Top 5 you'll recognize

4

Role · Production Company

Actually made the movie

This is the company that hired the crew, ran the set day to day, and answered for the budget. Often it's a shingle founded by a specific director, producer, or star — their in-house filmmaking operation.

"A Legendary Pictures Production"

Top 5 you'll recognize

3

Role · Financier / Co-Financier

Put up the money, not the crew

A financing partner covers some or most of the budget in exchange for a cut of the profits, but isn't on set making creative calls — closer to a silent investor than a filmmaker.

"In association with Village Roadshow Pictures"

Names to know

This role turns over faster than any other on this list — financing deals are often specific to one film, so don't expect the same names film to film.

2

Role · International Sales Agent

Sells the film abroad, territory by territory

Handles selling the right to release the film in other countries to local distributors — essential for a film that doesn't already have a global studio behind it. You'll rarely spot this one on the actual print; it lives on trade ads and posters at markets like Cannes and Berlin.

"International Sales: FilmNation Entertainment" (on the poster, not the film)

Top 5 you'll recognize

1

Not a company · Possessory Credit

"A Film By ___" is a person, not a company

This is the one that actually trips people up — it shows up in the exact same sequence as the company cards, in the same typeface, but it's naming the director (occasionally a producer) as the film's principal author. It's a negotiated contractual right, not a corporate credit at all.

"A Film By Denis Villeneuve"

Worth knowing

One company, many hats

  • NetflixFinancier, production company, and distributor — all three jobs, in-house.
  • A24Produces its own films and also distributes them itself.
  • Marvel StudiosA production company owned outright by its own distributor, Disney.
  • Blumhouse ProductionsProduction company whose whole business model is built around financing its own low-budget slate.
  • Legendary PicturesProduction company that also co-finances its own tentpoles alongside a studio partner.

Also worth spotting: "Filmed with the participation of ___" usually credits a production-services company or a regional tax-incentive partner that provided crew and facilities in a given country — a logistics role, not a financing or creative one.

Currently in motion: Paramount Skydance is in the process of acquiring Warner Bros. Discovery, a deal expected to close in Q3 2026. If it goes through, two of the five distributor names above end up under the same corporate roof — a reminder that this landscape keeps consolidating.
