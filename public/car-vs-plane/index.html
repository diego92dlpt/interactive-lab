Car vs. plane: CO₂ per person, New York to Frankfurt

If you could drive a straight road from JFK to Frankfurt, how would a car compare with flying? Pick a scenario, or open the settings to change any assumption.

Scenario explorer comparing CO2, energy or fuel per person for gasoline and diesel cars versus airliners on a New York to Frankfurt trip.

The short answer (default settings)

Per person, counting CO₂ only:

  • The newest twin-engine jets (787-9, A350, A330neo, A321LR) emit about 20% less than two people sharing a typical sedan, and 20–30% less than older widebodies such as the 767-300ER and A380. The sedan itself roughly matches the average widebody flight.
  • Driving alone emits roughly twice as much as flying: from about 1.8× the A380 to 2.5× the newest jets.
  • Four people in a midsize SUV come within a few percent (gasoline) to about 15% (diesel) of the newest jets in economy, and beat older jets such as the 767-300ER and A380.
  • A business-class seat on the newest jets emits about as much as driving alone; on older jets, more.

Counting non-CO₂ effects (the CO₂e measure, 2× for planes by default) changes the answer: in every scenario except driving alone, the car is level with or ahead of every plane. A solo driver still loses to most planes, and only matches or beats the oldest, such as the 767-300ER and A380.

Gaps of a few percent are ties. Sources and caveats are in the FAQ under Settings.
