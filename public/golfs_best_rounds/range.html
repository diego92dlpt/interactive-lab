The caveats ledger

Everything above should be read with these in hand. One place, linkable.

#qualification-paths · Country comparisons carry a selection effect: fields aren't sampled evenly. The Open admits local and regional qualifiers (diluting ENG and SCO with club-level rounds), while an American at Birkdale is almost always a travelling elite. A country's bar mixes "how good are its players" with "how its players got in."
#field-gaps · Older archives thin from the bottom: the 1983 Open here holds 122 of 151 players, and the missing names all missed the cut. Early championships therefore look slightly kinder than they were, and every early-years figure leans that way.
#no-amateur-flag · The data doesn't mark amateurs or ceremonial competitors. Their rounds are real and are counted; nothing separates a club champion qualifier from a tour professional.
#cut-truncation · Half the field has no weekend rounds. Any view touching R3–R4 describes survivors only — a better population than the one that teed off on Thursday. The cut filter makes this visible; it can't make it go away.
#par-1991 · The source data lists the 1991 Open at Birkdale as par 71; it was par 70, and every number here uses the correction. The only edit ever made to the source, and it lives in one place in the pipeline.
#small-n · Hollow marks mean the cell holds fewer than 25 rounds — enough to plot, not enough to lean on. The footer always reports the thinnest cell in view.
